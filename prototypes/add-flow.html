What this shows

Each note lists the handoff requirement the screen is standing in for.

Tap Add — three intents

  • Three first-class errands, ordered by how often each is the answer, not by hierarchy depth. decision 11
  • A newcomer takes the first and never reads the other two; a veteran goes straight to the one they meant. principle 1
  • Each intent commits on its own — the line under the list says so out loud.
  • Opening this from a crag or rock page skips it entirely and pre-fills the level above.

Add a problem — one sheet

  • One scroll, no steps. Going back is scrolling up, and nothing is discarded on the way. decision 14
  • The two context rows arrive answered: nearest spot, and the rock you were last at. Tapping change expands that picker inline and collapses it on pick.
  • Spot list is distance-sorted with a search box — "Citatah · 80 m" sitting above "Citatah 125" is the only duplicate-spot prevention in the design. open item 8
  • Rock picker is a photo grid, and photoless tiles identify themselves by the problems on them ("Slab Mantap, Sit Start") — never "Rock 3". principle 3
  • "It's a new rock" and "Not sure which rock" both proceed; re-parenting later is what makes that honest. principles 5, decision 13
  • The photo field is here, not on a rock step — you're standing in front of it holding a camera. It uploads to the rock. If the rock already has photos, it offers them to draw on instead.
  • "Draw the line" appears only once a photo exists, and never blocks submitting.
  • Name is the only required field — grade included. Submit stays disabled rather than enabled-and-scolding. Type a name to watch it wake up.
  • No word on this screen is technical: no "annotation", no "required field", no "parent". principle 7

… at a brand-new spot

  • The Rock row is gone entirely. When the spot is being created in the same breath there is nothing to choose between, so creating the rock is the app's job. decision 12
  • The person never sees the word "rock" as a question — only as "a photo of the rock".
  • The motivating case end to end: name the spot, drop the pin, name, grade, photo, done. Three fields and a photo, with all three levels filed behind the scenes.
  • Patokan and access notes are in "more details" — present, never in the path.

Add a spot

  • A short sheet that saves and stops. The result is a dimmed pin reading "no problems yet · add the first one". open item 1
  • This is the state the shipped wizard made unreachable — it committed nothing until a problem name existed.
  • Gets its own photo now: the approach shot, so cards show the place instead of borrowing a rock's picture. decision 2, amended

Add a rock

  • Same shape one level down, and there for the veteran — documenting a rock shouldn't require inventing a problem for it. decision 11
  • Batu / tebing is asked in plain words, and drives every "which rock?" vs "which wall?" string after it. The word "boulder" never appears. decision 1, principle 6
  • Name and stone type are optional and last — most rocks have neither.

After submit

  • "Add another to this rock" keeps the spot and the rock and clears only the climb fields. Documenting a rock means entering its lines one after another — sit start, stand start, the traverse — in one sitting.
  • Repetition, not confusion, is the veteran's failure mode. This is the single highest-value affordance in the flow and nearly free to build. principle 1
  • Same offer one level up after adding a rock.

Static prototype: the pickers, grade chips, disclosure and submit-gating are live; nothing saves. Fonts fall back to Georgia / system sans when offline. Not built from the app's components — per handoff.md's implementation note the real sheet should be built through the impeccable skill.
